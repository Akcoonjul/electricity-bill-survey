Before you begin

This is a final-year research project at the University of Technology, Mauritius. It has built a web application that estimates a household's monthly electricity bill from the appliances it owns and how often they are used. So far it has only been tested against simulated data. To find out whether it works on real Mauritian homes, it has to be compared against what households were actually billed.

The form takes about fifteen to twenty minutes and asks for:

  • the appliances in your home, and roughly how long each is used
  • the number of people living there
  • from one or more CEB bills: the period, tariff category, kilowatt-hours and total
What is never asked for. No name, no address, no CEB account number, no meter number, no email, no phone number. There is no field on this page for any of them. Your household is identified only by a code such as H01, which is given to you at the end, and nothing connects that code to you.

Where your answers go. When you press Submit, the answers on this page are sent to a Google Sheet owned by the researcher, and they are read only by him. Google acts as the storage provider and its own terms apply to that transfer. Nothing is stored in your browser, and no analytics, cookies or tracking are used on this page.

Taking part is voluntary and you may stop at any point simply by closing this page — nothing is sent until you press Submit. Once submitted, you can still withdraw by quoting your household code to the contact address shown at the end. Results are reported as totals and averages; no individual household will be identifiable in the dissertation. The data is used for this dissertation only and is deleted once it is marked.
